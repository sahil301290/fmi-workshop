The workshop vision

Great healthcare AI needs to make it beyond the paper.

Medical AI research is advancing quickly, and it is easier than ever to build promising prototypes. But the journey from a published model or proof of concept to something usable in clinical settings remains challenging.

The inaugural FMI Workshop creates a meeting point for people who have designed, built, evaluated, or deployed practical AI systems for medicine and healthcare. Our emphasis is on experience from the field: what was prototyped, what worked, what did not, and what it takes to translate an idea into impact.

Topics we want to explore.

From the first sensor reading to the final clinical workflow, the programme explores the decisions that make medical AI prototypes useful, reliable, and deployable.

01 /

Rapid medical AI prototyping

Fast iteration, proof-of-concept systems, low-code tools, standalone applications, and clinical decision-support interfaces.

02 /

Multi-sensor data & fusion

Combining physiological, environmental, behavioural, and contextual signals into actionable intelligence.

03 /

Wearables & connected health

Smart sensors, remote monitoring, edge AI, real-time pipelines, and continuous patient-centred data.

04 /

Trustworthy, safe & explainable AI

Reliability, transparent evaluation, privacy, bias, governance, and human oversight in medical AI systems.

05 /

Clinical workflow integration

Usability, interoperability, clinician feedback, integration with existing services, and implementation barriers.

06 /

From prototype to practice

Validation in real environments, evidence generation, translational partnerships, regulation, and scale-up.

Useful to know

Frequently asked questions.

Answers to the most common questions as we prepare for the inaugural workshop.

Is this a paper-submission conference?

No. This is an invitation-led, presentation-based workshop. We are not inviting proceedings papers or publishing a proceedings volume. Contributions can discuss previously published work or systems already in practice.

Is the MDPI Sensors Special Issue part of the workshop?

No. The related journal Special Issue is a separate publication opportunity. The workshop does not publish proceedings, and speaking at or attending the workshop has no bearing on journal peer review or editorial decisions. The published journal deadline is 31 March 2027, before the workshop date.

Can I apply to speak if I have not been invited?

Yes. Use the speaker expression-of-interest form for the academic, industry, or PhD / early-career lightning-talk track. We prioritise experience developing, testing, or deploying working medical AI prototypes. The organisers review proposals and invite selected speakers; submitting an expression of interest does not guarantee a place.

Will there be opportunities for PhD researchers?

Yes. The draft programme includes a lightning-talk segment for PhD and early-career researchers who have relevant prototypes or applied results. Expressions of interest are welcome through the same speaker form.

What is the finish time?

The published programme runs from 08:00 to 16:00 BST. The venue is planned to remain available until approximately 16:30 for informal discussions and event close-down, subject to the room booking.

Can I attend remotely?

The current plan is for an in-person event at Ulster University Belfast. A remote attendance option has not been announced.

Where can I register and pay?

Registration is not yet open. A link to a university-approved booking platform will be published here when arrangements are finalised. This static website does not collect payments or attendee data.

How can my organisation sponsor the workshop?

Industry and other external sponsors are welcome to enquire. Email the workshop chair using the link in the Funding & Partners section.
